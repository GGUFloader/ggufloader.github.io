AI in Legal Jobs: 7 Tasks Lawyers Can Automate Today

AI won't replace lawyers — but lawyers who use AI will replace those who don't. Here's where to start.

AI is already doing legal work — the question is who uses it well

Every week, legal teams are quietly handing tedious, high-volume work to AI: reading 400-page contracts, searching thousands of documents, drafting first-pass memos. The tools have matured enough that the bottleneck is no longer capability — it's knowing which tasks to automate and which app to trust with confidential client data.

This guide breaks down the 7 legal tasks AI can automate today, what each looks like in practice, and the recommended app for getting them done privately.

Task 1: Legal research with real citations

Instead of keyword-searching a document set and reading hits, lawyers now ask questions in plain English: "What are my obligations under this agreement?" The AI searches the actual files, retrieves the relevant passages, and answers with the exact source — document, page, and passage — so you can verify before you rely on it.

Task 2: Document review and summarization

First-pass review of discovery, due diligence, and deal documents is prime AI territory. A legal AI reads every page and produces structured summaries, flags anomalies, and groups documents by topic — turning days of junior-associate time into minutes.

Task 3: Contract clause screening

AI contract review scans an agreement and flags the clauses that actually matter: unlimited liability, auto-renewal, broad indemnification, missing termination rights. Each finding is rated High / Medium / Low risk with a plain-English explanation — a second set of eyes that never gets tired.

Task 4: Due diligence on deal documents

During M&A and financing work, teams upload a data room and ask targeted questions: "Which contracts require change-of-control consent?" The AI searches the whole set, answers with citations, and lets the team verify each one — dramatically cutting the due diligence timeline.

Task 5: Intake and matter triage

New client intake generates a flood of semi-structured documents. AI classifies them, extracts key facts (parties, dates, amounts), and drafts the intake summary — so a paralegal can spend time on the matter instead of data entry.

Task 6: Compliance checks against your own playbook

For repeatable work — jurisdictional rules, firm formatting standards, disclosure requirements — you define a playbook once and let the AI scan every document against it. Violations are flagged, rated, and explained automatically.

Task 7: First-draft memos and summaries

AI generates solid first drafts from your own documents — memos, chronologies, issue summaries — which a lawyer then reviews and refines. The key is that the draft is grounded in the source material, not hallucinated.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will AI replace lawyers?

Not in the foreseeable future. AI automates specific tasks — research, document review, contract screening — but legal judgment, strategy, negotiation, and client relationships remain human work. The lawyers who adopt AI will outwork those who don't; the job changes, it doesn't disappear.

What legal tasks can AI automate today?

The most mature use cases are legal research with citations, document review and summarization, contract clause screening, due diligence, intake triage, and drafting first-pass memos. Tools like Lawyer Assistant handle all of these locally on your own machine.

Is it safe to use AI for client work?

Yes, if you choose privacy-first tools. Cloud chatbots may train on or retain what you paste. Local-first apps like Lawyer Assistant keep documents on your machine — nothing leaves your computer in local mode — which is why they're recommended for confidential client work.

How do lawyers and paralegals get started with AI?

Start with one high-volume, low-risk task such as searching your own document set, then expand. Pick a tool with citations you can verify, run it on a pilot matter, and check every answer. The recommended starting point is a private, local legal AI app that reads your existing files.
